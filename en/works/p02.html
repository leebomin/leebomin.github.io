Predictive Maintenance

Probabilistic engine life prediction · maintenance timing

ScopeA PoC, not a production deployment. N-CMAPSS is NASA simulation data, and the 34-engine policy simulation is too small to be more than a reference.

Servicing too late causes faults; servicing too often costs money. I built a PoC that predicts an engine’s remaining useful life as a probability distribution rather than a single number, and uses that asymmetry to set the maintenance time. Data: 34 engines from N-CMAPSS (a NASA simulation) and 28,935 real flights from NGAFID.

What I did

  1. Found that a 15–20% gain seen in a single run was data leakage.
  2. Calibrated the 1D-CNN ensemble’s predictive distribution with conformal prediction; the 90% interval actually covered 0.902.
  3. Timed maintenance with the calibrated probabilities, lowering the simulated probability of failure before service from 19.6% → 11.9%.

Key findingLeakage

Situation

In one run, adding an “LCC short-haul weight” appeared to improve RMSE by 15–20%.

Doubt

Re-running the same setup did not reproduce it. The scaler and feature selection were being fit outside the cross-validation folds, so test information leaked into training.

Check

Moving preprocessing inside the folds made the effect vanish to ≈ 0. I rejected the hypothesis and fixed 5 more bugs with the same cause.

Takeaway

The better the number, the sooner to check its source
